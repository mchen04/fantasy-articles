Your league settings are your first scouting report. Read them before you open a ranking list. A useful draft plan answers one question: what does this league reward?

Redraft starts with a fresh roster each season. You do not need to preserve a keeper plan here. You need a team that fits the current rules and leaves room to change.

## Make a one-page rules sheet

Write down the scoring format, active roster slots, bench size, and eligible positions. Add the lineup deadline, acquisition limit, waiver process, and trade deadline. Use the settings inside your own league as the source of truth.

Keep this sheet beside your draft list. If a recommendation assumes different settings, mark that difference before you use it. A good player can still be the wrong answer to a particular roster problem.

## Separate scoring from convenience

Points leagues turn statistics into one score through assigned values. Category leagues compare the selected statistical categories. That difference changes how you compare players. Yahoo’s scoring overview explains the formats; your league supplies the actual settings.

Next, check how often you can change the lineup. Daily changes and a weekly lock create different workloads. Do not build a plan that depends on moves you cannot make.

## Count your usable roster spots

List each active slot and its position rules. Then consider where your first few picks can play. Flexible eligibility can help you fill a lineup, but a spare eligible position does not create another active slot.

Treat bench space as a limited resource. Decide how much room you want for dependable cover, uncertain upside, and short-term moves. You can revise that mix after the draft.

## Leave with three decisions

Before draft night, choose your scoring priorities, your approach to early uncertainty, and the bench role you want to keep flexible. Write a short reason for each choice.

This is a working plan, not a prediction. Revisit it when your roster changes. The rules stay useful even when a ranking list goes out of date.
