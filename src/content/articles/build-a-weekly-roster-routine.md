A good weekly routine reduces rushed decisions. Give yourself one place to check the lineup, one place to record a roster question, and one planned time to review both. The aim is a useful habit you can repeat.

This guide does not supply a live NBA schedule or injury report. Open current public sources and your league’s own settings before you act. Keep the date of your check beside any time-sensitive note.

## Start with the deadlines

Check when lineups lock, when waiver claims run, and when acquisition limits reset. Put the next relevant deadline in your calendar. Use your league’s displayed time zone instead of assuming it matches your own.

Then check the active lineup. Look for empty slots, position conflicts, and players whose availability needs a fresh check. Resolve those questions before spending time on a possible trade.

## Map games to open slots

Use the official NBA schedule to see when teams play. Compare those dates with your active roster. A scheduled game only helps your fantasy lineup if you can use the player in an available slot under your league’s rules.

Look at crowded dates and quiet dates separately. Avoid counting bench games as automatic gains. Write down the specific opening a potential move would fill.

## Choose one roster question

Pick the decision with the clearest effect on the coming week. It might concern cover for a position, a short-term opening, or a player whose role needs review. State the question in one sentence.

Separate what you know from what you expect. Check current public reports for availability. Treat possible changes as uncertain until the evidence supports them.

## Make a brief closing check

After the week, compare your plan with what happened in your own league. Did the move fill the intended slot? Did a deadline or rule surprise you? Record the lesson without turning every outcome into proof of a good or bad decision.

Keep the note short. Next week, begin with that note and the current deadlines. A routine becomes valuable when it helps you act calmly, then lets you get on with your day.
