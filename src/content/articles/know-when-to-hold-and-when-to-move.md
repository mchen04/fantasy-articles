A hold decision is still a decision. Review why you use the roster spot, then ask whether that reason remains supported. The goal is to react to meaningful change without chasing every box score.

This guide does not name current drops or holds. It gives you a consistent way to assess them. Use public, current evidence and your league’s settings for the actual choice.

## Return to the original role

Write down why you draft or add the player. Was the spot meant to cover a position, support a category, or test a larger opportunity? Compare the present situation with that original job.

A poor performance does not automatically remove the job. A lasting role change may. Keep those ideas separate so a disappointing result does not become a story unsupported by evidence.

## Check the opportunity before the outcome

Review current playing-time and role information from public sources. Consider whether the change appears temporary or sustained. Mark unknown details instead of filling them with a confident guess.

Basketball terms can help you frame the question. Usage describes a player’s share of team plays while on court. It does not alone settle fantasy value. The NBA glossary provides the definition; your roster needs provide the context.

## Compare the real replacement

A drop creates an opening for a particular available player, not an imaginary perfect option. Check what the replacement can do, when you can use that contribution, and what uncertainty comes with it.

Include waiver cost and the chance that you cannot recover the outgoing player. Then compare both uses of the spot over the same period. A short-term need can justify a move, but make that time horizon explicit.

## Set the next review point

If you hold, decide what evidence would change your mind. If you move, record the problem the replacement should solve. Both choices need a reason you can check later.

Avoid defending a draft cost that you cannot recover. Also avoid treating activity as progress. A calm decision with a clear review point gives you a better basis for the next move than a reaction you cannot explain.
