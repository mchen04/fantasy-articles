A useful trade improves the roster you can actually play. Start with the team’s needs and the rules. Then compare both sides using the same evidence and the same time horizon.

This guide offers a review method. It does not assign current player values or guarantee a fair exchange. Public information and your league’s specific settings must support the final decision.

## State the problem first

Describe what the trade should solve. You might need a better fit for an active slot, more dependable cover, or a different category profile. If the problem is unclear, the proposal may simply replace one uncertainty with another.

Keep the reason separate from a player’s reputation. A famous name can still leave the same lineup gap. A less celebrated option can have a useful role in your format.

## Compare complete roster effects

List the incoming players, outgoing players, and any extra drop needed to fit the deal. For an uneven trade, include the player you can add with a newly open spot or the player you must release.

Compare the active lineup after the trade with the lineup before it. In a category league, check the shape across categories. In a points league, use the actual scoring weights. Bench value matters, but it cannot all count as active production at once.

## Check uncertainty on both sides

Use current public reports for role and availability questions. Date your notes. Apply the same caution to players you want and players you already own.

Avoid treating one side’s best outcome as the expected outcome. Write a plausible concern for each side. If the deal only works when every uncertain detail breaks your way, recognize that dependence before accepting it.

## Make the offer easy to understand

Explain the roster fit you see for both teams in plain language. Do not invent urgency, claim private knowledge, or promise a result. Check league trade rules and deadlines before you submit.

After the decision, keep the original reason. Review that reason when new evidence arrives. One strong or weak performance does not settle the entire trade; it gives you another piece of information to consider.
