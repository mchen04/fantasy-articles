Streaming means using a roster spot for a short-term need. Begin with the need, then find an eligible player. Reversing that order can leave you with an appealing add who never reaches your active lineup.

This is a planning guide, not a list of current streaming targets. Confirm the official schedule, current availability, and your league’s acquisition rules before making a move.

## Find the usable opening

Write down the date, position, and active slot you want to fill. Check whether the slot is already occupied by a player you prefer. Count only the opportunities your rules let you use.

For weekly lineups, plan before the lock. For daily lineups, still check individual lock times. The chance to make a move does not guarantee the chance to use it.

## Match the player to the need

In a category format, identify the categories you want to support. In a points format, compare expected contribution through your actual scoring weights. Do not transfer a recommendation across formats without checking the fit.

Use current public information to assess the role. A recent scoring burst is a reason to investigate, not a complete case for an add. Look for evidence that the opportunity can continue during the dates you need.

## Include the cost of the drop

The outgoing player may offer future value or useful cover. Compare that cost with the short-term opening. Check whether waiver rules make the player hard to recover if you change your mind.

Also count the acquisition you spend. A limited move may be useful later in the week. Keep enough room for a foreseeable lineup problem rather than spending every move at the first opportunity.

## Define the exit before the add

Decide when you will review the short-term spot. The review can happen after the planned games or when the role changes. A stream can become a longer hold, but it should earn that change through evidence.

Afterward, ask whether the move created usable games and served the intended need. You cannot control every performance. You can check whether the plan used a real opening and paid a sensible cost.
