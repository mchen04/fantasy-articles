## Five days, 156 picks

The NBA Redraft board is final. Twelve teams, 13 rounds and 156 picks went into the books between Wednesday, October 7, when the draft start was recorded at 2:15 PM Pacific, and Sunday, October 11, when the final pick landed at 6:18 PM Pacific. The pick clock was set to 24 hours, which is generous enough to research a player, overthink him and take someone else.

Teams here are labeled by draft slot, so Team 1 picked first in round 1. This was a snake with a third-round reversal, which means the order ran from Team 12 down to Team 1 in round 2 and again in round 3 before flipping back.

## The first round

Team 1 opened with Nikola Jokić at No. 1. Luka Dončić went second to Team 2, Victor Wembanyama third to Team 3 and Shai Gilgeous-Alexander fourth to Team 4. Giannis Antetokounmpo was the first power forward off the board at No. 5.

Round 1 went four point guards, three centers, two power forwards, two shooting guards and one small forward. That lone wing was Jayson Tatum at No. 9, and through two full rounds he was still the only small forward taken. This league apparently decided wings are something you find later, like a parking spot.

The rest of the opening round included Cooper Flagg at No. 10 for Team 10 and Karl-Anthony Towns closing it out at No. 12 for Team 12.

## Point guards everywhere

Point guards were the board's favorite flavor: 42 of the 156 picks, more than any other position. Centers were next at 35, then shooting guards at 28, power forwards at 27 and small forwards at 24.

Team 11 committed early and often, opening with Tyrese Maxey at No. 11, Tyrese Haliburton at No. 14 and Trae Young at No. 26. Teams 2, 5, 6 and 11 each finished with five point guards. Twice the board produced three point guards in a row, at picks 97 through 99 and again at 112 through 114, which is the draft board version of everyone reaching for the same door handle.

Team 9 went the other direction and drafted six shooting guards: Donovan Mitchell, Devin Booker, Tyler Herro, Kon Knueppel, Josh Hart and Ayo Dosunmu. Nobody will ever accuse Team 9 of lacking a plan. Everyone will ask what it is.

## The reversal and the big men

The third-round reversal paid off for Team 12 right away. That slot turned into Karl-Anthony Towns at No. 12, Scottie Barnes at No. 13 and Evan Mobley at No. 25. Team 12 kept building tall from there, adding Joel Embiid at No. 73 and Myles Turner at No. 121, and finished with four centers and four power forwards.

At the other end, Team 1 picked back to back at No. 36 and No. 37, taking James Harden and Deni Avdija, after landing Stephen Curry at No. 24.

The busiest stretch for size came at picks 17 through 19, when Alperen Şengün, Anthony Davis and Domantas Sabonis went in a row to Teams 8, 7 and 6. Team 8 later added Chet Holmgren at No. 53 and ended with four centers, tied with Team 12 for the most.

## Late-round notes

Team 3 used its fifth and sixth picks, No. 58 and No. 63, on Cameron Boozer and Stephon Castle. The final pick, No. 156, sent Dillon Brooks to Team 1. That means Team 1 opened with Nikola Jokić and closed with Dillon Brooks, a range that covers roughly every mood the sport has to offer.

## The desk's opinion

This part is opinion, clearly labeled. The desk's favorite haul belongs to Team 12, because the reversal handed it three of the first 25 picks and it spent all three on size. The desk's most confusing haul belongs to Team 9, and the desk respects it anyway.
